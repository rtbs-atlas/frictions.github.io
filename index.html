Symposium Abstract

This symposium puts a focal point on the frictions that arise when the ambitions of science-making come into contact with the disciplines of the arts and architecture. With a primary focus on methodological questions, the symposium is organized in a series of workshops, walks, and ateliers. Seminars and lectures also aim to extend questions to consider the ethical and philosophical implications that arise when putting face to face the ways of working from a wide range of fields, and geographies.

There have been, and there are, different ways in which one conducts research and exercise knowledge production in respect to the so-called ‘environment’. There is classical Islamic philosophy on the one hand: Ibn Arabi and Ibn Khaldoun for example. But also early modern philosophers such as the Cartesians, Spinoza, Whitehead and others in recent history. The ‘turns’ that followed are those in Pragmatism, Science and Technology Studies—Dewey, Law, and Latour for example—but also decolonial thought and the feminist pragmatists on the other: Haraway, and Yusoff for example. We have moved as such, between dualistic conceptions of the study of environment, pre-colonial relational metaphysics, the relational materialism of posthumanism, and decolonial registers. With the recent works of this century, arts-based and artistic practices have found a firm place in science-making.

In this symposium, we want to focus on the contentious frictions that arise when different ways of working are confronted with each other, rather than aiming to ‘innovate’ or to draw on the overlaps and intersections of already rather widely practiced methods. Set participants include academics and post-graduate students from Universidad de Los Andes in Bogotá, Colombia, the University of Salford, and the Manchester School of Architecture, a joint school between Manchester Metropolitan University and the University of Manchester.
